## 1. Positioning and pastoral guardrails

Women should feel valuable, deeply loved by God, capable of faithful action, and respected as experienced adults. Business ownership can be a calling and a responsibility, but sales, earnings, and visible growth are not measurements of God's affection or approval. Never imply that paying Kingdom Solutions AI is an act of obedience to God. Invite women to steward their time, resources, experience, talents, and spiritual gifts wisely, with integrity and excellence.

Lead with their lived experience: revising the offer, posting more, buying more training, worrying about wasted time, and privately fearing that their experience may not be translating into a sustainable business. Then name the possibility of an identifiable business gap, not a character flaw.

The method is **Find the gap → Close the right gap → Build what's next**. "Build in the Right Order" explains how the business work proceeds, but is not the headline. The Direction Gap is the entry point; Offer, Audience, and Structure appear as possible additional diagnoses. The eight systems from the Handbook remain supporting context rather than a second major taxonomy.

## 4. Webinar run of show: 45-minute teaching and invitation plus 15-minute Q&A

The script below provides complete core passages and transitions; expand the examples with a **real, approved** business story from Tabitha's career. Never invent client outcomes. Keep slides simple and speak naturally. Presenter notes should show this entire thread.

### Slide 1 — Finding the Gap (0:00–5:00)

**On slide:** Finding the Gap / What's Keeping Your Business From Moving Forward? / Kingdom Solutions AI™ by Revived Spirit

**Opening passage:**

"Welcome. I'm Tabitha Rector, and I'm grateful you're here. Before we discuss your business, I want to remind you of something I believe matters more than any revenue number: you are dearly loved by God. Your worth is not determined by your sales, your following, or the speed at which your plans take shape.

"If you believe you've been called to business ownership, that calling is a privilege and a responsibility. It asks us to bring our skills, experience, spiritual gifts, time, and resources with wisdom and excellence. But it does not require us to pretend we know every answer.

"Today we're going to create some breathing room. We will look at the places where effort may not be producing results. Then we'll identify one gap that deserves attention first. My hope is that you leave clearer, lighter, and prepared for your next practical step."

**Prompt:** "Write down one word that describes how your business feels right now. No one else needs to see it."

### Slide 2 — The invisible cost of working harder (5:00–11:00)

**On slide:** You're putting in the effort. Why isn't it connecting?

**Passage:** "Maybe you've rewritten your website, joined another training, invested in tools, or posted faithfully without the response you expected. I understand why that can make you question yourself. It can even make you wonder whether you misunderstood the calling in the first place. But more work is not always the missing answer. Sometimes a single unresolved gap makes every other effort less effective."

**Reflection:** "Where has your effort increased without a clear result? Your message? Your offer? Reaching buyers? Delivering the work?"

Transition: "Let's begin with the Direction Gap because everything else needs something clear to point toward."

### Slide 3 — Direction: whom, what, and why now (11:00–19:00)

**On slide:** Direction Gap / Who do you serve? / What problem do you solve? / What is the first offer?

**Teaching passage:** "You may be excellent at many things. That is a strength—but a buyer cannot purchase a list of all the ways you might help. Clarity requires one understandable starting point. Ask: Who is this for? What urgent problem does this person recognize? What specific result can I responsibly help them work toward?"

**Exercise:** "Complete the sentence: I help [specific person] who is struggling with [recognized problem] so they can [meaningful next outcome]."

**Coaching caution:** Do not promise to make this perfect in a minute. It is a first hypothesis to test with real people.

### Slide 4 — Expertise: how I learned to see the real issue (19:00–23:00)

**On slide:** Experience helps us see beneath the symptoms

**Passage:** "My work has taken me through banking, lending, sales, marketing, business advisory, and coaching. I've helped bank branches generate millions of dollars, and I've coached teams to build stronger results. Working with owners also taught me that what appears to be the problem is often not the root of the problem."

**Required presenter insertion:** Tell one true, anonymized story about noticing an underlying direction, offer, audience, or structure problem. State the observable change without inventing a financial result. Point out the diagnostic question that led to the better decision.

### Slide 5 — Four gaps, one starting point (23:00–29:00)

**On slide:** Direction / Offer / Audience / Structure

**Direction:** Unclear buyer, problem, or primary offer.  
**Offer:** The value and buying decision are hard to understand.  
**Audience:** The right people are not entering useful conversations consistently.  
**Structure:** The right work lacks a reliable process.

**Passage:** "You do not need to fix four gaps today. You need to recognize which one is doing the most harm to the next step. An audience problem cannot be solved by posting more if the offer itself is still unclear."

**Reflection:** Ask participants to circle the one gap that feels most familiar, then check whether Direction comes before it.

### Slide 6 — Your first right step (29:00–36:00)

**On slide:** Name the gap → Describe the evidence → Choose one action → Set a 30-day check-in

**Guided exercise:**
1. Name the gap you believe leads.
2. Write one observable piece of evidence (not a judgment about yourself).
3. Choose one action small enough to complete this week.
4. Write how you will know whether it helped.

**Passage:** "Action without diagnosis is expensive. But diagnosis without action leaves us where we started. Your next step should be clear enough to take—and modest enough to learn from."

### Slide 7 — Build in the Right Order (36:00–39:00)

**On slide:** Diagnose → Clarify → Build → Activate

**Passage:** "This is what I mean by Build in the Right Order. We diagnose before adding another tool, clarify before expanding, build only what supports the priority, and activate a real path toward customers. AI may help with research and routine work, but it doesn't replace your authority, responsibility, or judgment."

### Slide 8 — The invitation: 30-Day Business Fast Track™ (39:00–43:00)

**On slide:** Clarity and implementation, with experienced guidance

**Invitation passage:**

"If today's exercise helped you recognize a gap, the next challenge is acting on what you've found. And you don't have to do that alone.

"I created the 30-Day Business Fast Track™ for women who are ready to turn their expertise into one clear offer, a sales-ready message, and a practical revenue path. We start with a private strategy and kickoff session, then work through four guided implementation sessions. You'll leave with a Business Priority Map, Signature Offer Blueprint, Sales-Ready Message Kit, and a 90-Day Roadmap, with Clarity Pro™ access and support as you implement.

"This is a small-group experience for up to eight women, and every enrollment begins with a Strategy Call. My Right Order Promise is simple: complete the first week's work, and if you still don't have a clear priority and plan, I'll provide a private realignment session at no additional cost.

"If you're ready to steward your next step with a real plan and accountable support, I'd welcome a conversation."

**CTA:** Book your Business Fast Track Strategy Call.

**Avoid:** Public price, false deadlines, guaranteed revenue, equating enrollment with obedience to God, or improvised bonuses that increase the fulfillment workload.

### Slide 9 — Close and live questions (43:00–60:00)

**On slide:** You are valuable. One gap. One faithful next step.

**Closing passage:** "I want you to remember that your business is something you steward; it is not the source of your worth. You are deeply loved, and you have skills and gifts worth using well. Take one next step from clarity. You don't have to build everything today. Thank you for spending this time with me."

Invite questions for the remaining time. If it fits Tabitha's delivery style, offer an optional brief closing prayer—but do not make prayer the condition for obtaining business help.
