## Slide 2. The effort that isn't connecting (5:00 to 11:00)

**Visible slide:**
You've been working. Why isn't it connecting?

**Say:**

"Perhaps you've spent months putting things together. You have knowledge that can help people. You've invested in yourself. Maybe you've changed the website three times. You've taken another class because you thought one more certification would make you ready. Or you've been told to post every day, be on more platforms, make more videos, and constantly show up.

"And somewhere in the middle of that, there's a quiet question: 'Why isn't this landing?'

"That question can become painfully personal.

"A woman who has succeeded in her career, solved difficult problems, coached other people, or carried responsibility for years may suddenly feel uncertain about how to explain her own business. That does not erase the experience she has earned.

"Sometimes the answer is not a greater amount of effort. Sometimes a gap in the business makes the effort less useful than it could be.

"Consider a woman who is excellent at what she does, but every time someone asks what her business offers, she gives a different answer. She has not failed in her field. She simply hasn't yet made one clear buying decision possible for the person listening.

"Now consider another woman who has a clear offer and a wonderful experience for her clients, but every conversation is with people who need a different service. She may work just as hard, yet her problem is different.

"And think about someone who has clients and demand, but she spends evenings catching up because no routine work has a repeatable process. Posting more will not solve her calendar or delivery problem.

"Those are different gaps. Each one asks for a different first step."

**Audience prompt:**

"Where have you put in real work without seeing the response you hoped for? Your message? The offer itself? Conversations with possible clients? Or simply keeping up with the work?"

**Pause:** 45 to 60 seconds.

**Transition:** "Let's start with the gap that affects many experienced women when they translate what they know into a business: Direction."

## Slide 3. The Direction Gap (11:00 to 19:00)

**Visible slide:**
Direction Gap

Who do you serve?  
What problem do they recognize?  
What is the first offer?

**Say:**

"You may have more than one gift. You may have spent years becoming competent at several things. That's wonderful. But a potential buyer needs to understand what problem you can help with today.

"When you're asked what your business does, does your answer become a long list? Do you offer a little coaching, some consulting, a program, maybe a membership, and something else you might create later?

"That makes perfect sense from your point of view, because you know the whole range of your experience. The person considering working with you does not yet know how to choose.

"Direction begins with a decision: who are you helping first, what problem are they trying to solve, and what is the clearest first offer you can make?

"Those answers do not have to define you forever. You can expand later. But we need a clear enough starting point to test.

"Let me give you an example. This is an illustration, not a client case.

"Imagine an experienced professional who can organize an office, manage people, improve processes, and train a team. Her website lists every one of those skills. A visitor thinks, 'She sounds impressive,' but still does not know whether to call.

"Now imagine that same professional says, 'I help small service businesses get client onboarding out of the owner's head and into a repeatable process.' The experience is the same. The first buying decision is easier to understand.

"That's the kind of clarity we're after. Not a catchy slogan for its own sake. A real person needs to recognize the problem.

"And because we are talking about stewardship, I want to add this: clarity can protect your capacity. When you know your first offer, you can stop building ten things at once. You can choose work that moves the business forward rather than constantly feeling behind."

**Guided writing exercise:**

"Take a moment and write a first draft of this sentence:

"'I help [the person] who is struggling with [the problem] so they can [the outcome].'

"Don't worry if it is awkward. We are testing a direction, not engraving a final mission statement."

**Pause:** About two minutes while people write. Invite one example if time permits. Ask, "Would your intended buyer recognize that problem in everyday language?" Never critique publicly with shame.

**Transition:** "That simple question reflects a lesson I've learned over more than thirty years of working with business owners."

## Slide 5. The four business gaps (23:00 to 29:00)

**Visible slide:** Four simple labeled blocks: Direction, Offer, Audience, Structure.

**Say:**

"I use four categories because they are understandable and useful. My Handbook goes deeper into the business systems. Today our goal is to identify a starting place.

"The **Direction Gap** appears when you are still deciding whom to serve, what problem to lead with, or which offer should come first.

"The **Offer Gap** appears when the direction is clearer but a buyer still cannot see the value of the specific service or what choosing it involves. People may compliment your work but hesitate at the point of purchase.

"The **Audience Gap** appears when the offer makes sense, but you're not consistently reaching or speaking with people who fit it. You may be visible, yet your visibility isn't leading to the right conversations.

"The **Structure Gap** appears when the work is real, perhaps even growing, but too much depends on you remembering everything or manually holding every step together.

"Notice how one can be mistaken for another.

"If I have a Direction Gap, I might buy a marketing course. If I have an Offer Gap, I might decide my audience isn't large enough. If I have a Structure Gap, I might assume I just need more self-discipline.

"That is why choosing the first gap matters. Wrong solutions cost time, money, and energy. Our goal is to be wise with all three."

**Interactive reflection:**

"Circle the gap that sounds most familiar. Then ask one more question: 'Could an earlier gap be the reason this one keeps appearing?'"

**Pause:** One minute.

**Transition:** "Now let's turn recognition into one doable action."

## Slide 6. Find your first fix (29:00 to 36:00)

**Visible slide:**
1. Name the gap  
2. Write the evidence  
3. Choose one action  
4. Check progress in 30 days

**Say:**

"I'm going to guide you through a short exercise. You do not need a sophisticated scoring tool or a lengthy workbook to begin.

"First, name the gap you believe deserves attention. Not the one that sounds the most impressive. The one that has evidence behind it.

"Second, tell the truth about what you're seeing. Maybe you have spoken with five potential buyers and three of them asked what exactly was included. That is information. Saying 'I'm terrible at business' is not information. It's a painful judgment about yourself, and it does not tell you what to fix.

"Third, choose one action you can complete soon.

"If Direction is your gap, talk with five people who fit the audience you want to serve. Listen for the problem in their words.

"If Offer is your gap, write a one-page description of the outcome, scope, and next step. Ask two trusted people what they think they would be buying.

"If Audience is your gap, identify five aligned connections or referral partners and begin genuine conversations. You do not need cold messages or automated pitches.

"If Structure is your gap, map one repeatable process that currently lives only in your head.

"Fourth, choose how you will check progress in thirty days. Did you learn more about the buyer? Can someone understand the offer? Did the right conversations begin? Is the process now repeatable?

"This is how we turn uncertainty into a next step you can actually take."

**Facilitation:**

- Give 90 seconds to name a gap and write evidence.
- Give 90 seconds to pick an action.
- Ask: "What will you do this week, and how will you know you completed it?"
- Invite two short chat replies if time allows. Keep a visible countdown so the offer segment starts on time.

**Transition:** "You have just experienced the beginning of what I call building in the right order."

## Slide 7. Build in the Right Order (36:00 to 39:00)

**Visible slide:**
Diagnose  
Clarify  
Build  
Activate

**Say:**

"Build in the Right Order is the method behind the way I work.

"We diagnose first. We ask what is truly happening.

"Then we clarify the choice that must be made. We do not let ten competing priorities lead the business.

"Then we build what supports that choice. It may be an offer description, a better follow-up routine, a client process, or a simple operating system.

"Finally, we activate it in real life. A clear offer has to be put in front of people. A process has to be used. A plan needs a check-in.

"You may hear a lot about adding AI to everything. I use AI where it genuinely supports the work, but I do not believe it should replace your judgment or authority. Technology can help you organize and follow through. You remain responsible for the decisions.

"For me, that is both sound business practice and faithful stewardship.

"And sometimes, knowing the right order is easier than implementing it consistently. That's where guided support becomes valuable."

**Transition:** "If you have identified a gap today and you want help closing it, I want to tell you about one way we can work together."

### Expected objections and gracious responses

**"I've already paid for coaching and it didn't work."**

"I understand why you'd be cautious. Let's talk about what you received, what was missing, and whether your situation calls for guided implementation now. I don't want to recommend another engagement unless we can identify specific work we would do differently."

**"I don't have time for a thirty-day program."**

"That is worth considering honestly. The Fast Track is designed around priorities, but you still have to make room to implement. A Strategy Call can help us decide whether the timing and workload are realistic."

**"I have too many gifts and interests to choose one direction."**

"You don't have to give up the others. We can select a first offer without making it your lifelong identity. The question is which problem you can responsibly help a clearly defined buyer solve now."

**"I'm afraid to invest and make another wrong choice."**

"You should make a careful decision. That's why enrollment begins with a Strategy Call. We'll review your goal, what the program provides, and what you would need to contribute. There should be no pressure to decide from fear."

**"What if I am not sure I'm called to this business?"**

"That deserves discernment and space, not a sales argument. We can discuss business decisions and practical readiness. You do not need me to tell you what God has or has not called you to do."
